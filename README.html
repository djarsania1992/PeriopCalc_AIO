Risk Calculators Overview

RCRI (Revised Cardiac Risk Index)

Clinical use: Predicts major adverse cardiac events (MACE) in non-cardiac surgery.

Interpretation:

  • Score range: 0–6 points
  • High-risk surgery input: intraperitoneal, intrathoracic, or suprainguinal vascular surgery
  • 0 points (≈0.4%): Low risk
  • 1 point (≈0.9%): Mildly increased risk
  • 2 points (≈6.6%): Moderate risk
  • ≥3 points (≈11%): High risk

Original / creation

  • Lee TH, Marcantonio ER, Mangione CM, et al. Derivation and prospective validation of a simple index for prediction of cardiac risk of major noncardiac surgery. Circulation. 1999;100(10):1043-1049. doi:10.1161/01.CIR.100.10.1043. PMID:10477528.
    Created the RCRI using 6 predictors and prospectively validated it in a separate cohort.

Validation / external validation

  • Ford MK, Beattie WS, Wijeysundera DN. Systematic review: prediction of perioperative cardiac complications and mortality by the revised cardiac risk index. Ann Intern Med. 2010;152(1):26-35. doi:10.7326/0003-4819-152-1-201001050-00007. PMID:20048269.
    Systematic review of RCRI predictive performance across noncardiac surgery cohorts.
  • Fronczek J, Polok K, Devereaux PJ, et al. External validation of the Revised Cardiac Risk Index and National Surgical Quality Improvement Program Myocardial Infarction and Cardiac Arrest calculator in noncardiac vascular surgery. Br J Anaesth. 2019;123(4):421-429. doi:10.1016/j.bja.2019.05.029. PMID:31256916.
    External validation of RCRI and NSQIP MICA in vascular surgery; both underestimated risk and had limited discrimination before recalibration.
  • Roshanov PS, Sessler DI, Chow CK, et al. Predicting Myocardial Injury and Other Cardiac Complications After Elective Noncardiac Surgery with the Revised Cardiac Risk Index: The VISION Study. Can J Cardiol. 2021;37(8):1215-1224. doi:10.1016/j.cjca.2021.03.015. PMID:33766613.
    Large prospective cohort evaluating RCRI performance for MINS, MI, cardiac arrest, and cardiac death.

Gupta MICA (Myocardial Infarction or Cardiac Arrest)

Clinical use: Estimates 30-day risk of myocardial infarction or cardiac arrest using age, ASA class, functional status, creatinine status, and a dedicated Gupta MICA procedure category.

Implementation note: This app uses a separate Procedure type (Gupta MICA) field because Gupta MICA requires standard procedure coefficients and should not be inferred from the RCRI or ARISCAT surgery fields.

Interpretation:

  • <1%: Low risk
  • 1–5%: Moderate risk
  • ≥5%: Higher risk

Original / creation

  • Gupta PK, Gupta H, Sundaram A, et al. Development and validation of a risk calculator for prediction of cardiac risk after surgery. Circulation. 2011;124(4):381-387. doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.110.015701. PMID:21730309.
    Developed the ACS NSQIP MICA calculator for perioperative MI or cardiac arrest using NSQIP data; validated in a subsequent NSQIP cohort.

Validation / external validation

  • Peterson B, Ghahramani M, Harris S, et al. Usefulness of the Myocardial Infarction and Cardiac Arrest Calculator as a Discriminator of Adverse Cardiac Events After Elective Hip and Knee Surgery. Am J Cardiol. 2016;117(12):1992-1995. doi:10.1016/j.amjcard.2016.03.050. PMID:27131613.
    External validation in elective hip/knee surgery; MICA showed good discrimination.
  • Fronczek J, Polok K, Devereaux PJ, et al. External validation of the Revised Cardiac Risk Index and National Surgical Quality Improvement Program Myocardial Infarction and Cardiac Arrest calculator in noncardiac vascular surgery. Br J Anaesth. 2019;123(4):421-429. doi:10.1016/j.bja.2019.05.029. PMID:31256916.
    External validation in noncardiac vascular surgery with systematic troponin surveillance.
  • Alrezk R, Jackson N, Al Rezk M, et al. Derivation and Validation of a Geriatric-Sensitive Perioperative Cardiac Risk Index. J Am Heart Assoc. 2017;6(11):e006648. doi:10.1161/JAHA.117.006648. PMID:29146612.
    Compared Gupta MICA with a geriatric-sensitive model in older adults; Gupta MICA had lower discrimination and underestimated risk in the geriatric subset.

DASI (Duke Activity Status Index)

Clinical use: Estimates functional capacity from patient-reported daily activities.

Note: DASI was originally created as a self-administered functional capacity instrument, not as a surgical event-risk calculator. It is now commonly used in perioperative cardiac risk assessment as an objective functional capacity measure.

Calculation: VO₂ peak = 0.43 × DASI + 9.6 (mL/kg); METs = VO₂ peak ÷ 3.5.

Interpretation:

  • ≥7 METs: Excellent functional capacity; generally low perioperative cardiac risk
  • 4–7 METs: Moderate functional capacity; intermediate risk
  • <4 METs: Poor functional capacity; higher perioperative cardiac risk

Original / creation

  • Hlatky MA, Boineau RE, Higginbotham MB, et al. A brief self-administered questionnaire to determine functional capacity: the Duke Activity Status Index. Am J Cardiol. 1989;64(10):651-654. doi:10.1016/0002-9149(89)90496-7. PMID:2782256.
    Created the 12-item DASI and validated it against measured peak oxygen uptake.

Validation / perioperative validation

  • Wijeysundera DN, Pearse RM, Shulman MA, et al.; METS Study Investigators. Assessment of functional capacity before major non-cardiac surgery: an international, prospective cohort study. Lancet. 2018;391(10140):2631-2640. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31131-0. PMID:30070222.
    METS study: DASI outperformed clinician-estimated functional capacity for perioperative risk prediction.
  • Riedel B, Li MHG, Lee CHA, et al.; METS Study Investigators. A simplified modified Duke Activity Status Index to characterise functional capacity: a secondary analysis of the METS study. Br J Anaesth. 2021;126(1):181-190. doi:10.1016/j.bja.2020.06.016. PMID:32690247.
    Evaluated shortened DASI versions against CPET-derived functional thresholds.
  • Silvapulle E, Darvall J, De Silva A. Association between the Duke Activity Status Index and complications after noncardiac surgery: A systematic review. J Clin Anesth. 2025;103:111808. doi:10.1016/j.jclinane.2025.111808. PMID:40101523.
    Systematic review associating lower DASI scores with postoperative complications, cardiovascular complications, and mortality.

ARISCAT

Clinical use: Predicts postoperative pulmonary complications (PPC) using age, oxygenation, infection, anemia, ARISCAT-specific incision/location, surgery duration, and emergency status.

Implementation note: ARISCAT incision/location is entered separately from the RCRI surgery category because the two tools define surgical risk categories differently.

Calculation: Sum points for each variable:

  • Age: 0 points for ≤50, 3 points for 51–80, 16 points for >80
  • Preoperative SpO₂: 0 points for ≥96%, 8 points for 91–95%, 24 points for ≤90%
  • Respiratory infection: 0 points for no, 17 points for yes
  • Preoperative anemia: 0 points for no, 11 points for yes
  • Surgical incision: 0 points peripheral, 15 points upper abdominal, 24 points intrathoracic
  • Duration: 0 points <2 hrs, 16 points 2–3 hrs, 23 points >3 hrs
  • Emergency: 0 points no, 8 points yes

Interpretation:

  • <26 points: Low risk — 1.6% in-hospital PPC
  • 26–44 points: Intermediate risk — 13.3% in-hospital PPC
  • ≥45 points: High risk — 42.1% in-hospital PPC

Original / creation

  • Canet J, Gallart L, Gomar C, et al.; ARISCAT Group. Prediction of postoperative pulmonary complications in a population-based surgical cohort. Anesthesiology. 2010;113(6):1338-1350. doi:10.1097/ALN.0b013e3181fc6e0a. PMID:21045639.
    Developed the ARISCAT score for postoperative pulmonary complications using 7 predictors and an internal validation split.

Validation / external validation

  • Mazo V, Sabaté S, Canet J, et al. Prospective external validation of a predictive score for postoperative pulmonary complications. Anesthesiology. 2014;121(2):219-231. doi:10.1097/ALN.0000000000000334. PMID:24901240.
    Prospective European external validation of the ARISCAT score.
  • Kokotovic D, Degett TH, Ekeloef S, Burcharth J. The ARISCAT score is a promising model to predict postoperative pulmonary complications after major emergency abdominal surgery: an external validation in a Danish cohort. Eur J Trauma Emerg Surg. 2022;48(5):3863-3867. doi:10.1007/s00068-021-01826-6. PMID:35050387.
    External validation in major emergency abdominal surgery.

STOP-BANG

Clinical use: Screening tool for obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) risk.

Interpretation:

  • 0–2/8: Low OSA risk
  • 3–4/8: Intermediate OSA risk
  • ≥5/8: High OSA risk

Original / creation

  • Chung F, Yegneswaran B, Liao P, et al. STOP questionnaire: a tool to screen patients for obstructive sleep apnea. Anesthesiology. 2008;108(5):812-821. doi:10.1097/ALN.0b013e31816d83e4. PMID:18431116.
    Original STOP tool paper; adding BMI, age, neck circumference, and gender produced the STOP-Bang extension with higher sensitivity for OSA.

Validation / external validation

  • Chung F, Subramanyam R, Liao P, Sasaki E, Shapiro C, Sun Y. High STOP-Bang score indicates a high probability of obstructive sleep apnoea. Br J Anaesth. 2012;108(5):768-775. doi:10.1093/bja/aes022. PMID:22401881.
    Surgical cohort validation against polysomnography; higher scores correlated with higher probability of moderate/severe OSA.
  • Nagappa M, Liao P, Wong J, et al. Validation of the STOP-Bang Questionnaire as a Screening Tool for Obstructive Sleep Apnea among Different Populations: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. PLoS One. 2015;10(12):e0143697. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0143697. PMID:26658438.
    Systematic review/meta-analysis across sleep clinic, surgical, and other populations.
  • Hwang M, Nagappa M, Guluzade N, Saripella A, Englesakis M, Chung F. Validation of the STOP-Bang questionnaire as a preoperative screening tool for obstructive sleep apnea: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Anesthesiol. 2022;22(1):366. doi:10.1186/s12871-022-01912-1. PMID:36451106.
    Preoperative/surgical population meta-analysis.
  • Vasu TS, Doghramji K, Cavallazzi R, et al. Obstructive sleep apnea syndrome and postoperative complications: clinical use of the STOP-BANG questionnaire. Arch Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg. 2010;136(10):1020-1024. doi:10.1001/archoto.2010.1020. PMID:20956751.
    Perioperative outcomes study linking high STOP-Bang risk to higher postoperative complications.

Caprini Score

Clinical use: VTE risk stratification to guide thromboprophylaxis.

Interpretation:

  • 0 points: Lowest VTE risk — minimal probability
  • 1–2 points: Low VTE risk — minimal probability
  • 3–4 points: Moderate VTE risk — approximately 0.7%
  • 5–6 points: High VTE risk — approximately 1.8%
  • 7–8 points: High VTE risk — approximately 4.0%
  • ≥9 points: Highest VTE risk — approximately 10.7%

Original / creation

  • Caprini JA, Arcelus JI, Hasty JH, Tamhane AC, Fabrega F. Clinical assessment of venous thromboembolic risk in surgical patients. Semin Thromb Hemost. 1991;17 Suppl 3:304-312. PMID:1754886.
    Original Caprini surgical VTE risk assessment model using a point-based risk-factor system.

Common modern version / update

  • Caprini JA. Thrombosis risk assessment as a guide to quality patient care. Dis Mon. 2005;51(2-3):70-78. doi:10.1016/j.disamonth.2005.02.003. PMID:15900257.
    Commonly cited source for the 2005 Caprini RAM used in many contemporary calculators.

Validation / external validation

  • Bahl V, Hu HM, Henke PK, Wakefield TW, Campbell DA Jr, Caprini JA. A validation study of a retrospective venous thromboembolism risk scoring method. Ann Surg. 2010;251(2):344-350. doi:10.1097/SLA.0b013e3181b7fca6. PMID:19779324.
    Large surgical inpatient validation using NSQIP-derived data.
  • Pannucci CJ, Bailey SH, Dreszer G, et al. Validation of the Caprini risk assessment model in plastic and reconstructive surgery patients. J Am Coll Surg. 2011;212(1):105-112. doi:10.1016/j.jamcollsurg.2010.08.018.
  • Shuman AG, Hu HM, Pannucci CJ, Jackson CR, Bradford CR, Bahl V. Stratifying the risk of venous thromboembolism in otolaryngology. Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg. 2012;146(5):719-724.
  • Obi AT, Pannucci CJ, Nackashi A, et al. Validation of the Caprini Venous Thromboembolism Risk Assessment Model in Critically Ill Surgical Patients. JAMA Surg. 2015;150(10):941-948. doi:10.1001/jamasurg.2015.1841. PMID:26288124.
  • Pannucci CJ, Swistun L, MacDonald JK, Henke PK, Brooke BS. Individualized Venous Thromboembolism Risk Stratification Using the 2005 Caprini Score to Identify the Benefits and Harms of Chemoprophylaxis in Surgical Patients: A Meta-analysis. Ann Surg. 2017;265(6):1094-1103. doi:10.1097/SLA.0000000000002126. PMID:28106607.

Features

  • Real-time calculation: scores update as you enter data.
  • No default values: each assessment starts with a clean slate.
  • Responsive design: works on desktop and mobile devices.
  • Persistent display preferences: text size and high-contrast mode are saved locally while calculator inputs still reset on refresh.
  • Validation warnings: incomplete or out-of-range inputs are flagged before scores are displayed.
  • Copy-ready risk assessment: results can be copied to the clipboard, with desktop hover preview.
  • Print-friendly results: print output focuses on the results panel.
  • Educational: quick reference interpretations accompany scores.
